How FindRoads Engine Works

FindRoads queries real-time OpenStreetMap road networks via the Overpass API across custom geographic bounding boxes or radius searches. It evaluates macro sinuosity ($S = L_{\text{arc}} / L_{\text{chord}}$), angular corner deflection density ($C$ in deg/km), speed suitability ($V$ centered around 50 mph), and penalizes traffic signals/junctions into a composite Fun Index score (0–100).
Unlike radius discovery which returns individual roads, Loop Route Builder performs graph pathfinding across the OpenStreetMap network graph. It anchors waypoints in circular quadrants around your starting location and computes shortest path circuits ($Start \rightarrow WP_1 \rightarrow WP_2 \rightarrow WP_3 \rightarrow Start$). Every leg connects end-to-end to form a continuous closed driving loop.
Patrol risk is calculated using OpenStreetMap MUTCD highway classification (major state motorways vs remote tertiary connectors), posted speed limits, junction density, and sinuosity curve shielding (continuous tight bends make stationary radar traps impractical). This is combined with regional citation indices derived from NY State DMV and NJ Courts open data.
When an OpenStreetMap road segment lacks a tagged maxspeed, FindRoads applies fallback speed limits derived from official US State Department of Transportation highway functional classifications (35 mph for tertiary connectors, 25 mph for residential streets, 50 mph for primary state routes) rather than flat estimates.
FindRoads extracts empirical OpenStreetMap tags for traffic signals (highway=traffic_signals), commercial land use & retail stores (landuse=commercial|retail, building=commercial, shop=*, amenity=*), and frequent commercial driveways (highway=service). Roads with high business density or traffic light frequency receive heavy score penalties (up to -50 pts), ensuring pure canyon passes and un-congested backroads rank higher than commercial strip mall corridors.
FindRoads exports 1-click Google Maps Tours (with waypoints ensuring Google doesn't reroute you onto straight interstates), 1-click Google Street View 360° panoramas, standalone GPX 1.1 tracks (for Garmin, InRoute, Scenic, Calimoto, Apple Maps), and KML files (for Google My Maps & Google Earth).
